Who is responsible for your data

Servicios Ya is a web service with its own accounts. You can sign in with a code sent to your email or to WhatsApp, with your Google account, or with a link sent by our WhatsApp assistant (the bot).

The person responsible for your data (the data controller) is Ezequiel Ellena, an individual based in Rosario, Santa Fe, Argentina, who decides why and how your data is used and handles your requests. You can write to Ezequiel at ezequielellena_003@hotmail.com.

Servicios Ya is at an early stage and doesn’t run on permanent servers yet. See Where your data is stored for where your data is today.

What data we handle and where it comes from

Data generated when you use the service

  • Code requests: which email or number a code was requested for, through which channel, when, whether it was sent, whether it was used and how many failed attempts it had. Also what it’s for: to sign in or to check an email address or number being added to an account; in that case, we record which account requested it. The code itself isn’t stored. We record the request even if that email or number has no account, and even when we don’t send the code: that way the limits work the same for everyone and nobody can find out which addresses have an account.

This is recorded even when that person has no account.
  • Session: the access grants issued when you sign in, with their status and dates (see Cookies).
  • Account controls: the date the account was created, whether it’s active, recent failed attempts and temporary lockouts, and who created or last changed it.
  • IP address for rate limits: we use it for a few minutes, in memory only, to limit the number of requests. It isn’t stored.
  • Technical logs and traces: they help us find problems. Logs record which operation ran, at which website address, and whether it failed. Diagnostic traces describe each request to the website: the address requested (which may include the internal ID of an account or a role, but not the values after the “?”), browser details (the “user agent”), the response and how long it took, along with the database queries and calls to Meta and Google that the request made. They’re designed to leave out codes, links, message text and email addresses, and logs show phone numbers masked. The exception is the database engine’s error log, which may include a value from the operation that failed.

How we protect it

  • Everything travels encrypted: between your browser and the service (HTTPS), and between the service and its providers.
  • No passwords: you sign in with a one-time code or link, or with Google.
  • Codes and links aren’t stored in our database: only a fingerprint (a “hash”) that lets us check them but can’t be turned back into the code or link. They expire after 10 minutes, work only once, and each code allows up to 5 attempts. The copy kept in Gmail of each email with a code does include it (see How long we keep it).
  • Limits: up to 5 codes every 15 minutes for the same email or number, a daily cap on WhatsApp codes and limits per IP address. They apply to every code, whether it’s to sign in or to add an email address or number. After 10 failed sign-in attempts in a row, the account is locked for 15 minutes.
  • Verified messages: we check Meta’s signature on every message coming in from WhatsApp and discard any without it.
  • Records without secrets: in stored messages, the code is replaced by the placeholder “[código]” and the sign-in link isn’t stored. The sign-in history keeps the reason a sign-in failed, never the code that was typed.
  • Sessions: the website’s credentials live in the tab’s memory, not in browser storage, and the session cookie can’t be read by the page’s code. When you sign out, one of them passes through the page address and may stay in your browser history (see Cookies and browser storage). If an account is deactivated or deleted, or if an administrator unlinks its WhatsApp, its sessions end immediately.
  • Permission-based access: only people with the right permission can see and manage accounts.
  • Keep your links to yourself: the sign-in link the bot sends shows, before you sign in, which account it belongs to (your name or email, and your masked number). Don’t share it.

Cookies and browser storage

The Servicios Ya website doesn’t use advertising or analytics cookies, and it doesn’t load anything from other sites: no fonts, no scripts, no pixels. It only uses what’s needed for you to sign in.

Cookies

  • Session cookie: keeps you signed in. It lasts 30 days, renews as you use the service and survives closing the browser. It’s deleted when you sign out. It’s only sent over HTTPS and the page’s code can’t read it.
  • Google sign-in cookies: two temporary cookies that protect the round trip to Google. They last a few minutes and are deleted when sign-in finishes.

Browser storage

  • Local storage (localStorage): your language and whether the side menu is open or collapsed. They stay until you clear the site’s data; signing out doesn’t remove them.
  • Tab storage (sessionStorage): temporary sign-in data: a security value, the page to return to and, if signing in with Google fails, the error code. It’s deleted once used or when you close the tab.
  • Tab history: the email or number you type to request a sign-in code is kept in that tab’s history (not in the page address) so it can be verified and resent. It goes away when you close the tab, unless your browser restores it.
  • Session credentials: they live in the tab’s memory and aren’t saved in browser storage. The exception is signing out: so the service knows which session to end, the website sends it the credential that says who you are (the “ID token”) inside the page address. That credential contains your user ID in the system, your email if you have one, your name (or, if you haven’t set one, your email or number) and your roles, so that address may stay in your browser history. It can’t be used to sign in to the service.

Since all of this is needed to sign in, we don’t ask for permission to use it. If you block cookies, you may not be able to sign in.

This page uses no cookies or scripts. It’s hosted on GitHub Pages, and GitHub may log the IP address of visitors.
